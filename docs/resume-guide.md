# Resume Writing Guide

A great resume is your ticket to an interview. This guide is designed to help college students craft a professional, ATS-friendly resume.

## 1. Resume Basics
- **One Page:** Keep your resume strictly to one page. Recruiters spend only a few seconds glancing at it.
- **Single-Column Layout:** Always use a single-column layout. Two-column layouts often fail Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) parsing.
- **File Format:** **ALWAYS export your final resume as a PDF.** Never send a DOCX or editable file unless explicitly asked.
- **Filename:** Use a professional filename: `Firstname_Lastname_Resume.pdf`. Not `Untitled_Document_123.pdf`.

## 2. Company Types & Targeting
- **Small Companies / Startups:** Often look for breadth of skills, quick learners, and evidence of building end-to-end projects.
- **Large Product Companies / MNCs:** Often prioritize deep understanding of core CS concepts (DSA, System Design), specific tech stacks, and scale.
- Tailor your highlighted projects or skills slightly depending on the role.

## 3. Formatting & Design
- **No Photos:** Do NOT include a photo on your resume unless you are applying in a specific country/industry that explicitly requires it. For standard tech roles, it's a hard no.
- **Fonts & Colors:** Use simple, professional fonts. Use a maximum of one subtle accent color (like dark blue).
- **No Graphics/Icons:** Avoid skill charts (e.g., a progress bar for "Java: 80%"). They are subjective and ATS systems cannot read them.

## 4. Personal Information
- **Must Include:** Name, City/State, Phone Number, Professional Email.
- **Links to Include:** LinkedIn, GitHub, LeetCode (or similar competitive programming profile). Keep these at the top!
- **Professional Email:** Use `firstname.lastname@gmail.com` or your college email. Avoid unprofessional addresses.
- **Do NOT Include:** Marital status, religion, exact home address, or Date of Birth (unless specifically required by an application portal).

## 5. Technical Skills
- **Be Honest:** **Don't claim technologies you cannot explain in an interview.** If you followed a tutorial for Docker but don't understand containers, don't list Docker.
- **Categorize:** Group skills logically (e.g., Languages, Frameworks, Databases, Tools).
- **Soft Skills:** Avoid listing generic soft skills like "Leadership" or "Teamwork" as bullet points. Instead, provide *evidence* of these skills in your Experience or Leadership sections.

## 6. Education
- List your most recent degree first.
- Include your university name, location, degree (e.g., Bachelor of Technology in CSE), expected graduation date, and CGPA.
- **Backlogs:** Be honest about active backlogs if asked during the process, but you generally don't need to list them on the resume itself.
- **Academic Achievements:** Mention significant academic ranks or scholarships if relevant.

## 7. Projects
Your projects prove you can build things. The descriptions should answer:
- *What did I build?*
- *How did I build it?*
- *What technologies did I use?*
- *What was the result or impact?*

**Important Rules for Projects:**
- **Keep it concise:** Use bullet points. Do not write paragraphs.
- **Tutorials/Clones:** If you built a project from a tutorial, **describe what YOU added, changed, enhanced, or optimized.** Do not falsely claim originality for standard tutorial clones.
- **Algorithms:** Mention algorithms or complex techniques *only when you genuinely used them*. (e.g., don't claim you used machine learning if you just wrote an IF statement).
- **Link it:** Always include a link to the GitHub repository or the live deployed project.

## 8. Experience (Internships / Work)
- If you have internships, list them in reverse chronological order (newest first).
- **Internships vs. Training:** Note that training programs or courses are *not* internships. Only list actual work experience or internships under this section.
- Focus on what you achieved, not just your responsibilities.
- Use action verbs (Developed, Engineered, Optimized, Designed).
- If possible, include metrics (e.g., "Reduced load time by 20%").

## 9. Other Sections
- **Achievements:** List relevant hackathon wins, coding competition ranks (e.g., Global Rank on LeetCode), or significant certifications.
- **Custom Sections:** Feel free to include Open Source Contributions if you have actively contributed to projects.
- **Leadership / Extracurriculars:** Mention your role in technical clubs, organizing events, or leading teams to demonstrate soft skills.

## 10. Section Ordering
Put your strongest sections higher up. A common order for college students is:
1. Header & Links
2. Education
3. Technical Skills
4. Experience (if any)
5. Projects
6. Achievements / Leadership

## 11. Final Review
- **Read your resume:** Read every single word before you send it.
- **Check links:** Click every link in your exported PDF to make sure they work.
- Use the [Resume Checklist](resume-checklist.md) provided in this repository!
